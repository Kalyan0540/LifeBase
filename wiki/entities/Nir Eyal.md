# Nir Eyal

Author, speaker, and behavioural design researcher. Best known for *Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products* (2014), which synthesised academic research on habits, operant conditioning, and motivation into a practical product design framework. Background includes teaching at Stanford Business School and building venture-backed companies.

## Key contributions in this wiki

- **[[Hook Model]]** — the four-step framework (trigger → action → variable reward → investment) for designing habit-forming products
- **[[Variable Rewards]]** — the application of Skinner's variable schedule of reinforcement to product design; three types: tribe, hunt, self

## Core ideas

The one reason anyone uses any product: to modulate mood. Internal triggers (negative emotions) are more powerful and more overlooked than external triggers (notifications). Reducing friction (ability) is higher ROI than increasing motivation. Products that appreciate with use (through stored investment) create switching costs that transcend feature comparison — this is why the best product doesn't always win.

## Follow-up work

*Indistractable* (2019) — the flip side: how to resist habit-forming products that work against your own interests and goals.
