# Longevity Exercise Pillars

A balanced longevity exercise program trains multiple body systems rather than optimizing only for aesthetics, weight loss, strength, or cardio.

## The five-pillar model

### Strength

Strength training builds and preserves muscle, which supports independence, joint stability, bone density, insulin sensitivity, and metabolic resilience. The bone-density benefit connects to [[Bone Remodeling and Mechanical Loading]]: repeated loading can gradually reinforce bone where stress is applied.

Baseline pattern: three strength sessions per week, built around compound push, pull, and squat patterns, with progressive overload.

### Low-intensity cardio

Zone 2 cardio trains aerobic capacity and mitochondria without requiring exhaustion. It should feel conversational.

Examples: walking, jogging, cycling, swimming. The source's target is 150 minutes per week.

### High-intensity cardio

High-intensity work trains VO2 max and cardiovascular ceiling. It should feel hard enough that speaking comfortably is difficult.

Examples: sprinting, hard cycling, battle ropes, or Norwegian 4x4 intervals.

### Mobility and flexibility

Mobility keeps joints and connective tissue usable. Small daily practice can protect the ability to move freely as the body ages.

Focus areas: hips, spine, ankles, hamstrings, shoulders.

### Balance

Balance supports coordination, neuromuscular control, fall prevention, and aging resilience. Simple single-leg drills can be practiced daily.

## Connection to metabolic health

Exercise is not just calorie burn. Muscle contractions help clear glucose from the bloodstream, and repeated exercise improves insulin sensitivity. This connects the exercise model directly to [[Diabetes and Metabolic Health]].
