# Conflict De-escalation and Frame Control

Conflict de-escalation starts by identifying the ego threat underneath the visible argument. Frame control is the skill of keeping the conversation oriented around process, clarity, and shared outcome instead of accepting hostile assumptions.

## Ego Threat Triangle

The source describes three threat types:

- **Identity**: "Do not threaten who I believe I am."
- **Control**: "Do not take away my agency."
- **Safety**: "Do not make me feel socially unsafe, small, or excluded."

Escalation becomes more volatile when multiple threats activate at once.

## Diagnosis

Identity threat sounds like justification, reputation defense, moral superiority, or "that is not who I am."

Control threat sounds like "you are not listening," "let me finish," or resistance to being directed.

Safety threat looks like sarcasm, posturing, distancing, status sensitivity, or passive aggression.

## De-escalation Moves

- Reflect identity without accepting false claims.
- Give choices or process control when agency is threatened.
- Reassure shared standing when safety is threatened.
- Ask clarifying questions about loaded words.
- Redirect from blame to what happens next.
- Answer the emotional question underneath the literal question when useful.

## Pre-framing

Hard conversations work better when the frame is set early:

- define the goal as solving the problem, not winning
- keep both people on the same side of the table
- set boundaries against shouting, insults, or character arguments
- keep the scope small enough to finish
- preserve agency by saying what is open for the other person to decide

## Caution

This model is useful for reading conflict, but it can become manipulative if used to corner or dominate people. The healthier use is to lower threat while keeping boundaries clear.
