# Bone Remodeling and Mechanical Loading

Bone strength changes through remodeling. Bone is continually broken down and rebuilt, so repeated mechanical loading can gradually reinforce the parts of the skeleton that experience stress.

## Remodeling is active

Bone is not a static material. Osteoclasts break down bone tissue, while osteoblasts rebuild it.

This cycle lets the skeleton adapt to the demands placed on it across life.

## Wolff's law

Wolff's law says bone remodels in response to mechanical loading.

The source uses a callus analogy: repeated friction thickens skin in a local area. Bone responds to repeated loading in a similar location-specific way, but more slowly and deeply.

## Exercise implication

Loading the skeleton through exercise can help bone become better matched to the forces it regularly faces.

The adaptation is long-term. The source frames bone strengthening as something that occurs over years of repeated loading, not from isolated exercise sessions.
