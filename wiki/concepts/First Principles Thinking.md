# First Principles Thinking

A reasoning approach that strips a problem down to its irreducible fundamentals — the things known to be true — and builds upward from there, rather than reasoning by analogy from existing solutions.

> "Boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there." — Elon Musk

## Reasoning by analogy vs. first principles

Most people reason by analogy: look at how a problem has been solved before → make incremental improvements. This produces at best slight variations on existing approaches and is bounded by the ceiling of the original paradigm.

First principles reasoning: identify the assumptions embedded in the current framing → break them down to fundamental truths → create new solutions unconstrained by the prior paradigm.

The [[Airplane and Rocket Analogy]] captures this: if your goal is the moon, optimising an airplane eventually reveals that the physics are wrong. You need a different vehicle.

## Three steps

1. **Identify and define current assumptions** — list every assumption embedded in how you currently frame the problem. What are you taking for granted?
2. **Break down to fundamental principles** — strip assumptions away; find the irreducible components. What do we *know* is true?
3. **Create new solutions from first principles** — reason upward from the fundamentals without the constraints of the prior paradigm.

## Musk examples

- **Tesla batteries**: conventional wisdom was that batteries cost $600/kWh. First principle: what do the raw materials actually cost? Answer: a pathway to $80/kWh and a target of $0.80.
- **SpaceX rockets**: assumption = rockets are single-use. First principle: why can't you land and reuse a rocket? Result: reusable launch vehicles, massive cost reduction. Rocket scientists at NASA couldn't see this because the assumption was invisible.
- **Tesla manufacturing**: assumption = car manufacturing lines run at the same rate by design. Musk questioned this; result: factory producing cars at walking pace with a target of running pace.

## The mental model angle (Douglas Adams)

[[PDC 1996 Keynote with Douglas Adams|Douglas Adams]] makes the same point from a perceptual angle: humans don't perceive reality directly — they maintain a **mental model** and update it with incoming data. The biscuit story is the comedic extreme: Adams was so committed to his model (the stranger is stealing *my* biscuits) that he never questioned it until he found his own unopened packet under his newspaper. First principles thinking is the discipline of surfacing and questioning the model itself.

## Business applications

- **Real estate fund**: questioning whether ownership is required to run an Airbnb-style operation → partner with an existing fund or bank REO inventory instead
- **Dugout mugs**: questioning whether discarded baseball bats are required as raw material → source raw wood at 10× lower cost
- **LLM wiki**: questioning whether RAG re-derivation is the right knowledge retrieval model → build a persistent, compounding wiki instead. See [[LLM Wiki Architecture]].
