# AI Writing Signals

AI-assisted writing can become recognisable when it repeatedly chooses inflated, generic, or unsupported language over plain and specific wording.

## Common Signals

- **Inflated verbs:** “serves as” and “features” replace simpler statements using “is” or “has.”
- **Manufactured drama:** rhetorical fragments and superlatives turn ordinary facts into revelations.
- **Vague authority:** unnamed experts or industry reports are used instead of traceable evidence.
- **Generic praise:** stock phrases such as “rich cultural heritage” or “breathtaking” substitute for concrete description.
- **Formulaic contrast:** “it is not X, it is Y” reframes ordinary things as sweeping transformations.
- **Style clustering:** noun-heavy sentences, low pronoun use, em dashes, and fashionable words recur together.

## How to Use the Signals

Treat them as revision cues rather than a detector. A single word, punctuation mark, or sentence pattern does not establish that text was written by AI. The useful questions are:

- Can the sentence say the same thing more plainly?
- Can generic praise be replaced with a concrete observation?
- Can an authority claim name and link its source?
- Is the dramatic framing supported by the importance of the fact?
- Do several formulaic patterns repeat across the same passage?

## Limitation

Writing conventions change once people become aware of them. Human writers may avoid supposed “AI words,” while models can be prompted to avoid the same markers. The signals therefore describe a moving style pattern, not a stable authorship test.
