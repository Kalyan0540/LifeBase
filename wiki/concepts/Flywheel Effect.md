# Flywheel Effect

A business strategy concept originating from Jim Collins's *Good to Great* (2001), describing how positive feedback loops build momentum and increase the payoff of incremental effort over time.

> **Flywheel Effect:** Positive feedback loops that build momentum, increasing the payoff of incremental effort.

The metaphor: a massive, heavy metal flywheel disk is hard to start spinning. Each push barely moves it at first. But the wheel's momentum builds — and eventually it spins with unstoppable force, requiring no more effort per push than the first rotation.

## Four components

1. **Momentum** — objects in motion tend to stay in motion (Newton's First Law applied to business). A flywheel that's already spinning is far easier to accelerate than one at rest.
2. **Feedback loops** — the faster it spins, the easier it is to add incremental speed; the more energy generated, the more excitement builds.
3. **Compounding return on effort** — no single push does it. Continuous small inputs accumulate into large outputs over time.
4. **Directional focus** — sustained effort must remain focused in one direction. Misplaced effort wastes or undoes momentum.

## The Flywheel as meta-competitive advantage

The Flywheel is not itself a competitive advantage — it is a **force-multiplier** on whatever advantages you already have. It accelerates economies of scale, brand, and especially network effects.

Network effects are the most powerful source of Flywheel momentum because past a tipping point they become **auto-catalytic** — the output of the reaction becomes its own catalyst, creating a runaway loop with no ceiling.

## How to push it

- Choose the right direction early (changing direction resets momentum)
- Focus effort on the moves that spin the wheel fastest; eliminate the rest
- Make progress visible (people join winning streaks)
- Seek additional positive feedback loops wherever possible
- Find every incremental gain (in compounding games, small gains pay off for years)
- Stay patient — the strategy looks slow at first

## Examples

- **Amazon**: price → selection → convenience → customers → third-party sellers → lower costs → lower prices → loops
- **Uber**: more riders → shorter pickup times → more riders; more drivers → more coverage → more riders
- **Atlassian**: inbound-only sales → focused on making existing customers successful → success stories attract new customers → stronger product → more success stories
- **Disney**: entertainment → parks → merchandise → entertainment
