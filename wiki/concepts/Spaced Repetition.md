# Spaced Repetition

A learning method that combines active recall with expanding review intervals to make useful knowledge durable.

## Core mechanism

1. Learn an item.
2. Attempt to retrieve it without looking at the answer.
3. Review it near the point where recall is difficult but still possible.
4. Increase the next interval after successful recall.
5. Shorten the interval after failure.

Successful retrieval slows future forgetting, so the review gaps can grow over time.

## Active recall over recognition

Rereading often creates familiarity: the material looks known while it is visible. Active recall tests whether the learner can produce the answer without the cue. The retrieval effort is part of what strengthens the memory.

## Desirable difficulty

Reviews should be neither immediate nor impossibly late. The useful zone is “almost forgotten”: enough difficulty to require reconstruction, but not so much that the memory has disappeared.

## Card design

Strong cards are:

- **Small:** one idea per card.
- **Connected:** linked to examples, context, senses, related knowledge, or personal experience.
- **Meaningful:** chosen because they support a real goal, project, skill, or relationship.

Repeated failure is feedback to rewrite the card, not merely repeat it more aggressively.

## Practical starting rule

Begin with a small daily load, such as five new cards, and connect reviews to a stable cue. Scale only when the accumulated review burden remains sustainable.

## Best use

Spaced repetition works best inside a larger loop:

**doing → getting stuck → learning → remembering → doing again**

It preserves the building blocks needed for reasoning and creation; it does not replace practice, understanding, or application.
