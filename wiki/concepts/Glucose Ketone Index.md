# Glucose Ketone Index

The Glucose Ketone Index, or GKI, is a ratio between blood glucose and blood ketones. In [[Thomas Seyfried]]'s metabolic-health framework, it is used as a practical marker for whether the body is in a high-glucose state, a prevention-oriented metabolic state, or a deeper nutritional ketosis state.

## Calculation

The source describes the basic calculation as:

1. measure blood glucose
2. convert glucose from mg/dL to mmol/L by dividing by 18
3. divide converted glucose by blood ketones in mmol/L

Newer meters may calculate the ratio automatically.

## Source interpretation

The interview's chart frames GKI zones this way:

- very high GKI: higher-risk metabolic state associated with inflammation, obesity, cancer, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, dementia, and other chronic disease risks
- moderate GKI: moderate-risk state
- lower yellow/green range: prevention zone
- deeper green range: management zone for people using metabolic therapy under care

This is Seyfried's interpretation of the metric. It should not be treated as a diagnosis.

## Behavioural use

The source presents GKI as feedback, not as a rule imposed from outside. The idea is that a person can see how food, fasting, exercise, sleep, and stress affect their glucose/ketone state.

The source connects lower GKI to:

- lower glucose availability
- higher ketone availability
- healthier mitochondrial energy use in normal cells
- less favorable fuel availability for cancer cells in Seyfried's cancer model

## Cautions

The source repeatedly says people with cancer, diabetes, hypertension, carnitine deficiency, or other comorbidities need medical supervision before trying ketogenic diets, fasting, or intensive metabolic therapy.

GKI is a measurement tool. It is not a standalone treatment plan.
