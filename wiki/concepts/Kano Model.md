# Kano Model

A framework developed by Noriaki Kano for understanding how investment in product quality maps to customer satisfaction. Introduced to UX practice largely through [[Jared Spool]]'s work. Plots investment (x-axis) against satisfaction from frustration to delight (y-axis), revealing three distinct curves.

## The three curves

### 1. Performance payoff
Linear return: more investment → proportional satisfaction gain. Feature quantity lives here. Risk: unchecked feature growth causes **experience rot** — increasing complexity degrades the overall experience and creates design debt. The antidote is curation — saying "no" to features aggressively.

### 2. Basic expectations
High investment, low satisfaction ceiling. Customers don't consciously notice when these are met (hot water in a hotel shower arrives instantly); they notice immediately when they fail. Critically: **you don't get to set basic expectations — the market sets them for you.** Netflix set its own expectation (wide catalogue) then failed to meet it during licensing disputes, creating frustration it created for itself.

### 3. Excitement generators
Low investment, disproportionately high delight. The source of competitive differentiation and the most asymmetric return in design. These are additive — you have to *add* something, not just remove friction.

## The frustration → usable → delight arc

- **Frustrating → Usable**: remove bad things (subtractive design, classic usability work)
- **Usable → Delightful**: add something good (additive design, a fundamentally different challenge)

"Not sucky" ≠ "delightful." They are different processes. The Hyatt hotel case: random acts of generosity couldn't compensate for broken air conditioning and sloppy room service because generosity is an excitement generator, and excitement generators cannot substitute for failed basic expectations.

## Connection to MAYA

The [[The MAYA Principle]] provides a constraint on excitement generators: they must sit within the user's acceptance range. An excitement generator that is *too* unfamiliar becomes alienating rather than delightful.

## User journey mapping tool

Used alongside [[User Journey Mapping]] — mapping all customer touchpoints on the frustration-to-delight scale to identify where investment has highest impact.
