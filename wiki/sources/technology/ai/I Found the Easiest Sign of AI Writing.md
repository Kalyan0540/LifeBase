# I Found the Easiest Sign of AI Writing

Short-form video listing recurring stylistic cues that can make AI-assisted writing feel generic or synthetic.

## Signals Named in the Source

- Replacing plain verbs such as “is” or “has” with inflated constructions such as “serves as” or “features.”
- Using dramatic question-and-answer fragments to manufacture excitement.
- Making vague appeals to authority, such as “industry reports suggest” or “experts have noted,” without naming a source.
- Overloading sentences with nouns while avoiding simple pronouns.
- Reusing exaggerated phrases such as “rich cultural heritage,” “breathtaking,” and “mind-blowing.”
- Using the negation formula “it is not X, it is Y” to make ordinary claims sound transformative.
- Repeating stylistic markers popularly associated with AI, including em dashes and words such as “delve” or “intricate.”

## Practical Takeaway

These cues are most useful as editing prompts, not proof of authorship. When several appear together, check whether the writing can be made more direct, specific, and evidence-based. Vague research claims should be traced to named sources rather than accepted at face value.

The source also describes a feedback loop: once readers associate a word or punctuation habit with AI, human writers and researchers may start avoiding it. That makes any fixed checklist less reliable over time.
