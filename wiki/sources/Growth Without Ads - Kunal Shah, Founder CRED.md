# Growth Without Ads — Kunal Shah, Founder CRED

[[Kunal Shah]] presents the [[Delta 4 Framework]] in the context of CRED's growth — explaining why companies with high efficiency delta grow without advertising, while low-delta products require constant ad spend and discounting.

## The human efficiency drive

Humans are unique among animals in their drive to constantly increase efficiency. Every tool, product, and service represents an attempt to hack this drive. The entire product economy exists because humans are always seeking to do things better, faster, or with less effort.

## The Delta 4 Framework

For any product, score the old behaviour and the new behaviour out of 10. When the delta between old and new states is **≥ 4**, the product unlocks three compounding effects:

1. **Irreversible behaviour** — users will not go back. The new behaviour becomes the default.
2. **High tolerance** — users will tolerate glitches and gaps (e.g., continuing to use Ola even when maps fail) rather than reverting.
3. **UBP (Unique Brag-worthy Proposition)** — users spontaneously tell others. They gain social currency by sharing the discovery. The product spreads like a magic trick, not an ad.

## Examples scored in the talk

- **Old taxis → Uber/Ola**: roughly 3 → 8 = Delta 5. Irreversible. High tolerance. UBP.
- **Offline shirt shopping → Online shirt shopping**: roughly 6 → 5 (for many users). Below Delta 4. Reversible. No tolerance. No UBP. Requires discounts and cashbacks to artificially inflate perceived delta.
- **Food delivery (Swiggy/Zomato)**: Delta 4 for urban tech-savvy users, but not for users who already have two familiar restaurants on speed-dial. Explains rapid early growth followed by plateau.

## The advertising insight

Companies that don't achieve Delta 4 need ads, discounts, and cashbacks to manufacture demand. Companies that do achieve Delta 4 spread through UBP — word of mouth driven by social currency. **TrueCaller** is a clear example: it spread because early users could perform what felt like a magic trick (look up any phone number's owner), not because of any ad campaign. CRED itself: at the start of the talk, only 1–2 people in the room knew about CRED from an ad.
