# Building a Winning UX Strategy Using the Kano Model — Jared Spool

[[Jared Spool]] presents the [[Kano Model]] as a strategic framework for moving users from frustration to delight. Talk given at USI Events conference.

## Core argument

Satisfaction is not the goal — **delight** is. Satisfaction is merely the absence of frustration. The Hyatt Hotel case study illustrates this: the CEO's "random acts of generosity" programme failed because it tried to paper over broken basics (slow elevators, sloppy room service) with occasional perks. You cannot delight your way out of unmet expectations.

## The customer journey map

Map every touchpoint on a scale from extreme frustration to extreme delight. This reveals where investment will have the most impact. Moving from frustrating to usable requires removing friction (subtractive). Moving from usable to delightful requires adding something new (additive) — these are fundamentally different design challenges.

## The three Kano curves

Developed by Noriaki Kano, the model plots investment on the x-axis against satisfaction on the y-axis, revealing three distinct patterns:

1. **Performance payoff** — linear: more investment yields proportional returns. Feature count lives here. Risk: feature bloat causes *experience rot* — complexity degrades the overall experience, creating design debt.
2. **Basic expectations** — high investment, low satisfaction ceiling. Customers don't even notice these when they work (hot water in a hotel shower); they only notice when they fail. You cannot set these expectations — the market sets them for you.
3. **Excitement generators** — low investment, high delight potential. These are the source of competitive differentiation and the most asymmetric return on design effort.

## Feature bloat and experience rot

The pattern: small app with three features succeeds → enterprise customers request more → features multiply → UI complexity explodes → new users can't navigate → design debt accumulates. Smart companies study what users actually use, then prune everything else. The ability to say "no" to feature requests is a core design skill. Curators beat accumulators.

## Basic expectations

These are invisible when met and catastrophic when missed. Netflix's transition to streaming broke the expectation it had set itself (wide catalogue), frustrating users who couldn't find movies — a self-inflicted expectation failure. Apple Maps placing a phantom airfield in Airfield, Ireland is a classic example of failing a basic trust expectation.
