# You're Exercising Wrong

A longevity-focused exercise explainer arguing that durable fitness depends on a balanced combination of training systems, rather than choosing one internet-favoured modality.

## Main claim

Exercise is non-negotiable for healthspan, but the best program is not one single form of training. The body is made of multiple interacting systems. Ignoring strength, endurance, intensity, mobility, or balance creates long-term weakness elsewhere.

The source organizes longevity exercise into five pillars.

## Five pillars

### Strength training

Muscle health is presented as a major predictor of longevity and independence. Strength training supports joints, insulin sensitivity, bone density, metabolism, and the ability to function in old age.

Simple guide from the source:

- aim for at least three strength-building sessions per week
- use compound movements: push, pull, squat
- progressively overload weight or reps over time

### Low-intensity cardio

Low-intensity cardio, especially Zone 2, is framed as heart and metabolic training. It improves mitochondrial function, energy efficiency, fat oxidation, and metabolic health.

Practical cues:

- Zone 2 should allow conversation
- the source's target is 150 minutes per week
- walking, jogging, cycling, and swimming all count
- a 10-minute walk after meals can support blood sugar control

### High-intensity cardio

High-intensity cardio, zones 4 and 5, is framed as a way to expand cardiovascular capacity and improve VO2 max. The source treats VO2 max as a strong longevity marker.

Examples:

- sprinting
- cycling intervals
- battle ropes
- Norwegian 4x4 intervals: 4 minutes hard, 3 minutes easier, repeated four times

### Mobility and flexibility

Mobility determines whether the body stays functional. The source recommends daily work on hips, spine, ankles, hamstrings, and shoulders.

The practical threshold is small: 5 to 10 minutes daily can compound over time.

### Balance

Balance is treated as an overlooked marker of neurological health, coordination, and aging resilience. The source recommends daily balance drills, controlled movement, and single-leg stands.

## Practical synthesis

The source's model is not perfection or maximal intensity; it is consistency across systems. A balanced longevity program includes:

- strength for independence
- low-intensity cardio for metabolic and mitochondrial health
- high-intensity cardio for VO2 max
- mobility for usable range of motion
- balance for stability and fall prevention
