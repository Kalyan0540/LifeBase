# Flywheel Effect — Why Positive Feedback Loops are a Meta-Competitive Advantage

Eric Jorgenson's deep-dive into the [[Flywheel Effect]] concept originating from Jim Collins's *Good to Great*, exploring how positive feedback loops compound into durable competitive advantages.

## Definition

> **Flywheel Effect:** Positive feedback loops that build momentum, increasing the payoff of incremental effort.

In plain terms: when good things you do lead to more good things "just happening." The flywheel metaphor — a massive, heavy metal disk that's hard to start spinning but, once moving, generates its own momentum — captures four intertwined ideas: momentum, feedback loops, compounding return on effort, and sustained directional focus.

## The Flywheel as meta-competitive advantage

The key insight: the Flywheel Effect is not itself a source of competitive advantage. It is a **force-multiplier** of existing advantages. It accelerates and compounds whatever moat you already have. This makes it a "meta-competitive advantage."

## The Flywheel interacting with competitive advantages

- **Supply-side economies of scale** — Disney's empire: theme parks fund films fund merchandise fund parks. Each part feeds the others.
- **Brand** — College basketball recruiting: winning attracts better players, who win more. Each success makes the next success easier.
- **Network effects (demand-side economies of scale)** — Network effects and the Flywheel are commonly confused but distinct. Network effects are the most *powerful* source of Flywheel momentum because past a tipping point they become auto-catalytic: the output becomes its own catalyst. Facebook's global dominance is explained by this runaway loop.

## Examples

- **Amazon** — price, selection, convenience → more customers → more sellers → lower costs → lower prices → loops back
- **Uber** — more riders → shorter pickup times → more riders; more drivers → more coverage → more riders
- **Atlassian** — no outbound sales; enterprise team is purely inbound/upsell. Growth comes from one focused customer acquisition engine, with the entire company aligned to push the same flywheel.

## How to push the flywheel

1. **Pick direction early** — changing direction resets momentum
2. **Focus efforts** — find and double-down on the moves that spin the wheel fastest; eliminate or fold in the rest
3. **Make progress visible** — people join winning streaks; showing momentum recruits more effort, which creates more momentum
4. **Look for additional feedback loops** — small viral loops (a stamp on a coffee cup, an Instagram-worthy drink) add incremental spin
5. **Find every incremental gain** — in a compounding game, small gains pay off for years
6. **Keep pushing** — patience is the meta-skill; the strategy looks slow at first
