# My App Makes $50K/Month: This Is My ASO Playbook

Starter Story interviews Sebastian Röhl about the [[App Store Optimization]] practices he used for HabitKit. He presents ASO as a long-term system built on three foundations: keyword placement, screenshot conversion, and ratings and reviews.

The revenue, acquisition, ranking, and timeline figures below are speaker-reported examples from his apps, not independent benchmarks.

## Why ASO matters

ASO determines whether an app appears near the top of App Store searches or remains difficult to discover. Sebastian says roughly 98% of HabitKit users find it through the App Store or Google Play without other marketing.

ASO has two linked jobs:

1. Help the store understand which searches the app matches.
2. Convert the people who see the listing into downloads.

## Foundation 1: optimise keywords

The app name and subtitle are presented as the strongest keyword placements.

- Research the terms people actually use before choosing the app name.
- Use an ASO tool to validate search popularity and ranking difficulty; LLMs can help brainstorm but do not validate demand.
- Put the primary keyword in the app name. HabitKit uses “Habit Tracker - HabitKit.”
- Use the subtitle for secondary terms without repeating words from the name.
- Use the App Store Connect keyword field for additional terms: comma-separated, no spaces, no duplicates from the name or subtitle, no unnecessary plural variants, and no competitor names.
- Prefer a difficult but valuable primary keyword over an easy keyword with little search volume.

## Foundation 2: make screenshots convert

Users decide quickly whether a listing is relevant, so the first screenshot should show the app’s strongest and most distinctive feature.

- Show real product UI rather than generic welcome screens, abstract graphics, or lifestyle imagery.
- Make the value of the app understandable from the screenshots.
- Test listing variants instead of assuming more polished creative will perform better.

Sebastian describes an Apple Product Page Optimization test where older, less polished HabitKit screenshots converted better than redesigned ones. His lesson is that authentic and clear can outperform visually refined.

## Foundation 3: build ratings and reviews

Ratings and reviews provide social proof, influence conversion, and are presented as ranking inputs for Apple and Google.

- Ask for a review at a successful moment, such as immediately after a user completes a meaningful action.
- Do not ask during onboarding or after a failure.
- If a user dismisses the prompt, wait before asking again.
- Reply to reviews, thank positive reviewers, and help users reporting problems.
- Add a gentle review request after a successful support interaction.
- Treat repeated review themes as product research and prioritisation signals.

Sebastian reports that HabitKit accumulated more than 7,400 iOS ratings with a 4.8 average and more than 10,000 Google Play ratings with a 4.6 average.

## ASO compounds slowly

The source frames ASO as a long game rather than a launch tactic. HabitKit launched in November 2022 and remained difficult to find despite applying the basics. Sebastian reports reaching the top 10 in some non-US markets after about six months, appearing in the US top 10 after about a year, and reaching a consistent US top-five position after nearly three years.

The practical system is iterative: improve metadata, screenshots, review volume, and the product itself; ship updates; measure results; and keep working over a long enough period for ranking and conversion gains to compound.
