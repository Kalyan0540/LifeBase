# Why Your Tendons Never Heal (And How To Fix Them)

A Build With Science video about tendon pain, load management, and isometric exercise. It argues that tendons adapt more slowly than muscles, so sudden training increases and unprepared momentum can create a mismatch between muscular strength and tendon capacity.

## Main claim

Persistent tendon pain is often framed as a load-capacity problem rather than a reason for complete rest. The source says that avoiding all load may reduce pain temporarily while leaving the tendon underprepared for returning to activity.

Clicking by itself is not presented as proof of injury. Clicking accompanied by pain is treated as a stronger reason to assess load and seek appropriate care.

## Load management

Two training mistakes receive the most attention:

- increasing training volume or intensity faster than the tendon can adapt
- using momentum, bouncing, or rebound forces that the tendon has not been prepared for

The video offers an approximate guideline of avoiding jumps greater than 30% at once. This is a rule of thumb from the source, not an individualized medical prescription.

## Isometric exercise

The proposed rehabilitation tool is an isometric: contracting a muscle while holding the joint still. The exercise should resemble the movement that causes symptoms so the relevant tendon receives load.

Examples include:

- a static chest-press, push-up, or wall-press position for pressing-related shoulder pain
- a mid-curl or fixed pulling position for elbow pain
- a knee-extension hold or wall-based knee-extension variation for knee pain

The source's basic protocol is three holds of at least 30 seconds. It suggests starting twice daily for pain relief, then continuing three to four times per week for four to eight weeks, with short maintenance sessions afterward.

## Pain guidance and limits

The video says discomfort during an isometric may be acceptable around 3–4 out of 10 or lower if symptoms are not worse the next day. If pain increases or remains worse, reduce the weight or effort.

This is general educational material, not diagnosis or individualized treatment. The video itself advises consulting a physician before beginning an exercise program. Persistent, severe, traumatic, or worsening pain needs professional assessment.
