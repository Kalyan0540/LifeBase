# How Bones Get Stronger With Exercise

Short anatomy explainer on how bone adapts to mechanical loading. The source uses a dissected upper-thigh cross-section to explain cortical bone, bone remodeling, and Wolff's law.

## Core idea

Bone is living tissue, not a static structure. It is continually broken down by osteoclasts and rebuilt by osteoblasts.

This remodeling lets the skeleton adapt to repeated demands across life. Loading the skeleton repeatedly over time can reinforce the areas that experience the greatest stress.

## Cortical bone

The dense outer shell of the femur is cortical bone, also called compact bone. This thick compact layer gives the femur much of its strength.

## Remodeling cells

Bone remodeling depends on two complementary cell roles:

- Osteoclasts break down bone tissue.
- Osteoblasts rebuild bone tissue.

The source frames strength as the result of ongoing remodeling rather than a fixed property.

## Wolff's law

Wolff's law is the principle that bone remodels in response to mechanical loading.

The source compares this to a hand callus: repeated friction thickens skin where the stress occurs. Bone adapts similarly, but deeper and much more slowly.

## Practical implication

Repeated loading from exercise can make bone better able to resist the forces placed on it, but the adaptation happens over years rather than immediately.
