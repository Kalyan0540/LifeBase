# Airplane and Rocket Analogy

A short Notion note containing the **airplane and rocket analogy** — a mental model for understanding why incremental improvement within an existing paradigm cannot solve problems that require a fundamentally different approach.

## The analogy

Goal: reach the moon. Before rockets existed, the natural instinct was to improve the best existing solution — the airplane. Engineers built heavier motors, achieved greater altitude and speed, and kept optimising. But they eventually understood that an airplane, no matter how improved, cannot escape gravity. The physics are wrong. You need a different vehicle: a rocket.

## The insight

Incremental optimisation within a paradigm has a ceiling. When the ceiling is below what you need to achieve, the right move is not to push harder on the current approach — it's to change the approach entirely. This is the difference between the airplane mindset and the rocket mindset.

## Connections

This is a compact expression of [[First Principles Thinking]]: the airplane approach is reasoning by analogy (improve what exists); the rocket approach is reasoning from fundamentals (what does the physics of the problem actually require?). It also connects to the [[UX Design Maturity Model]] — organisations that cannot move beyond incremental feature-adding (airplane thinking) get disrupted by companies that redesign the experience from scratch (rocket thinking, e.g., Nest vs. Honeywell).
