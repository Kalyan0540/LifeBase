# How to Use First Principles Thinking for Business

A short workshop-style talk introducing [[First Principles Thinking]] as a practical tool for business problem-solving, illustrated through Elon Musk's Tesla/SpaceX decisions and two audience case studies.

## The maze metaphor

The talk opens with a maze exercise: most people solve it the fastest "conventional" way — but never consider going over the top if it's 3D, or exiting from a different edge if the constraints allow. The lesson: **it's our unexamined assumptions that limit us**, not our intelligence. First principles thinking is the discipline of surfacing and testing those assumptions.

> "It's the fricking assumptions that kill us."

## What is first principles reasoning?

Elon Musk's formulation: *"Boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there."*

By contrast, most people reason by analogy — they look at how a problem has been solved before and make incremental variations. Analogy-based reasoning produces incremental improvement. First principles reasoning can produce step-change innovation.

Practitioners throughout history: Aristotle, Euclid, Copernicus, Nikola Tesla, Thomas Edison, Richard Feynman, Elon Musk.

## Three steps

1. **Identify and define your current assumptions** — List every assumption embedded in how you currently frame the problem. For a business challenge, this means asking: what are we taking for granted about how this has to work?
2. **Break the problem into fundamental principles** — Strip away the assumptions; find the irreducible components. Musk's battery example: the cost per kWh was $600 when he started Tesla. He asked what the raw materials cost, not what the market charged for assembled packs. Result: a pathway to $80/kWh (and a target of $0.80).
3. **Create new solutions from first principles** — Reason upward from the fundamentals. SpaceX: rockets cost tens of millions each; what if you reuse them? This was obvious once you questioned "rockets are single-use" as an assumption, but rocket scientists at NASA couldn't see it because the assumption was invisible.

## Business case studies from the talk

- **Jerry's luxury real estate fund**: assumption = you need to own properties to rent them on Airbnb. First principle: Airbnb itself owns no properties. Solution: partner with a fund that already has deployed capital, or approach banks sitting on REO inventory.
- **Chris's dugout mugs**: assumption = must use discarded baseball bats as source material (costs $13.50/unit). First principle: the product is a wooden mug with nostalgia branding. Solution: source raw wood separately and manufacture at ~$1.35/unit — 10× cost reduction.
