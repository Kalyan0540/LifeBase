# Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products with Nir Eyal (Long)

A full workshop-length version of [[Nir Eyal]]'s [[Hook Model]] talk, delivered in Salt Lake City, March 2019. Covers the same four-step model as the short version but includes extended Q&A and discussion of tech ethics and Eyal's follow-up work on distraction. Sponsored by Bluehost.

## What is a habit?

A **habit** is a behaviour done with little or no conscious thought. Roughly half of everything we do each day is habitual. Eyal's thesis: we are at the beginning of an era where this same psychology can be used for *good* — helping people exercise, save money, be more productive — not just to maximise engagement for its own sake.

## The Hook: four-step model

> A hook is an experience designed to connect the user's problem to your product with enough frequency to form a habit.

Successive cycles through the hook shape customer preferences and harden habits over time.

### Step 1 — Trigger

- **External triggers**: environmental cues (notifications, ads, buttons) with the action embedded in the trigger itself
- **Internal triggers**: memories and associations inside the user's head. Most powerful triggers are **negative emotions** — boredom, loneliness, uncertainty, fatigue. Products that relieve these emotions win. Facebook → loneliness. Google → uncertainty. YouTube/Reddit → boredom. Email → the desire for completion/control.

### Step 2 — Action

The simplest behaviour done in anticipation of a reward. BJ Fogg's formula: **B = MAT** (Behaviour = Motivation × Ability × Trigger). Product teams default to boosting motivation (testimonials, videos), but the higher-ROI lever is **ability** — making the behaviour easier. Six ability factors: time, money, physical effort, brain cycles, social deviance, non-routine.

### Step 3 — Variable Reward

Not enough to satisfy — must leave the user wanting more. BF Skinner: variable schedule of reinforcement (not fixed) produces the highest rate of response. Three types:
- **Tribe**: social validation, competition, empathy — the social feed
- **Hunt**: search for information or material resources — the infinite scroll, slot machines
- **Self**: mastery, completion, control — clearing email, levelling up in games

### Step 4 — Investment

Users put in time, data, content, social connections, or reputation. This loads the next trigger and stores value that makes the product better with use — the opposite of physical goods that depreciate. The product becomes harder to leave as stored value accumulates.

## Ethics note

Eyal distinguishes between using the Hook Model to help users improve their lives vs. manipulating them. His follow-up book (*Indistractable*) addresses the other side: how to resist habit-forming products when they work against you.
